# Development guide

## Run and test

- Static site, no build step: `python3 -m http.server 8000` from the repo root, then open `http://localhost:8000`.
- Three.js r128, its post-processing examples, and astronomy-engine 2.1.19 load from CDNs (see `index.html`); three-mesh-bvh is vendored in `js/vendor/`. For offline or headless tests, route those URLs to local copies (`npm pack three@0.128.0 astronomy-engine@2.1.19`).
- Headless Chrome needs `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader` for WebGL; expect about 8 fps, so flights take tens of seconds.
- Debug helpers in the console:
  - `jumpTo(frame, new V3(), D)` teleports the camera; frames are in `FR` (for example `FR.earth`, `FR.die`, `FR.cell`).
  - `goToNode(id)` flies to a topic and opens its card; `showBodyNode(id, null)` opens a card only.
  - `openLab('circuit', 'thevenin')` and `openTracks()`.

## Picking, the body and flights

- **When picks happen.** The hover (highlight and tooltip) is worked out once the pointer has moved and then rested for 70 ms, or when what is under a resting pointer changed (`viewChanged()`: frames arriving, layers peeling), never while the camera moves. A wheel burst keeps its zoom pivot and picks again at most every 150 ms. Picks are accelerated (bounding trees per mesh, per-instance bounds, a sphere per frame): see Performance.
- **A fair pick (`pickAt`, js/engine.js).** What is seen under the pointer is what a click selects, the hover names and a zoom goes to. Two rules add a few rays, only where they can matter. (1) *A hairline in front of something larger*: when the nearest hit is a co-rendered thing and another thing lies behind it on the ray, and the triangle hit is under a pixel and a half wide, eight rays on a 3 px circle are cast at the near thing alone; if at most three touch it (`pickThin`: a stem gives two, a bee seen from 5 m none) the thing behind wins. (2) *A near miss*: when the ray finds nothing, the backdrop or the ground (priority 0 or less: `pickWeak`), rings of 5 and 10 px are tried against the co-rendered things whose bound the ray passes (`pickNear`); the first ring that touches one decides, nearest to the eye first, and only in front of the ground that was hit. `best.ndc` is the ray that hit. Cost: nothing where no thing is near; `t_perf_pick.py` holds the budget.
- **Name labels.** A label is its thing, except where a model of the park (a co-rendered thing, the body) is drawn under the pointer: then the click is on that model (`addLabelTo`). The names of the buttercup and the bee float over the dog in the start view.
- **Pick rule per frame.** `pickFrame` collects every hit; a frame may define `f.chooseHit(cands, forZoom)` to choose (default: highest `priority`, then nearest). Whatever it chooses, a hit on what a cut-open view has cut away (`f.coreClip(point)`) is no hit: nothing is drawn there (the dog's "Inside the eye" cuts the near eyeball away whole). The body frame (`bodyChooseHit`, `js/body_core.js`) takes the nearest part whose layer is showing (opacity at least 0.35), else the nearest drawn; markers (priority 3 or more) keep priority; hidden parts have `visible = false` and so cannot be picked.
- **Core sample.** `bodyCoreSample(F, pick)` casts the click's ray through every body part and returns the stack from the skin inward; the card lists it (`coreHtml`). Region of a skin point: `bodyRegion`: the segment of the nearest skeleton vertex (head, neck, arm, leg ...), and for the torso surface-anatomy bands read from the skeleton meshes: chest above the costal margin (sternum tip at the midline, sloping to the lowest front rib at the side; the 0.06 m above it is "lower chest"), abdomen below it down to the top of the hip bone, then lower abdomen and pelvis; at the back upper back down to the foot of the thoracic spine, lower back to the base of the lumbar spine, then buttock; "shoulder" above y 1.4 m and beyond 7 cm from the midline. Face normals are unoriented, so `bodyRegion` turns the normal toward the viewer (the last pick ray) before deciding front or back. `harness/core/t_regions.py` checks 55 points, front and back, that the region name and the stack under it agree.
- **Overlapping child frames.** `reanchor` enters a child via `pickChild`: the one on the current flight's path, else a frame whose `core` radius holds the target (smallest first), else best fit. A frame with `enterOnly = true` (every cut-open organ view) is entered only when it is on the current flight's path ("Look inside", double-click on a visible organ, a ruler landmark, `goToNode` of the organ or one of its hotspots): scrolling never enters it, zooming out or the breadcrumb leaves it, and scrolling back in does not re-enter it. While a flight runs, children of the flight's target frame that are not on its path are not entered (flying out of the atom to its parent no longer dives back in). `Frame.floorD` (metres) is a zoom floor: the camera cannot sink below it by scrolling (body 1.5 mm, which is inside the tissue frame's 2 mm; organ views 5% of the organ radius). During a flight, an anchor that is off the flight's path but whose parent is on it is left (siblings are reachable). `Frame.center` offsets the content centre from the frame origin.
- **Auto layers (the descent).** `bodyAutoUpdate` (`js/body_core.js`, called from the body's `F.update`, every ~110 ms) casts the camera's view ray (through the zoom target, which is the point under the cursor while you scroll) through every body part: `bodyRayStack`. `bodyPeel` turns the stack into peel amounts: a layer (skin, then muscle, then vessels, then bone) goes see-through when the camera is closer to its surface than `PEEL_K` (12) times the gap to the next deeper kind of layer behind it (at least 8 mm), each deeper layer waits for the one in front (half the distance), and the first organ, vessel or nerve on the ray is never peeled: the descent ends on the surface of what you point at. Because the distances come from the stack, hand, forearm, head and abdomen each peel at their own scale. The skin is a 2 to 3 mm double-sided shell, so a camera inside it is detected by counting passes. Per-class opacities (`peelOpacities`) ease toward the target in `applyMode`; the Auto button names the layer in focus. Hovering never pulls a ghost layer back (a ghost is not highlighted or outlined). Vessels (`VESSEL_ID`) are a layer of their own between muscle and bone. `bodySurface` gives every body part a faint object-space grain so close-ups of smooth parts are legible. `harness/core/t_descent.py` scrolls with real wheel events at 14 places.
- **Reveal (`goToNode`, search, chips).** A body part with its own cut-open view: fly into that view. A hotspot: fly into its organ's view at the hotspot. Any other part with a mesh: stay in the whole-body view, set `FR.body.reveal = {id}` (everything in front of the part along the view ray is peeled, organs in front are ghosted, the part is solid and highlighted), face it from the side it lies on and fly to its centre. `GOTO_NEAR` maps mesh-less nodes (thyroid) to the part they sit against. Cleared by closing the card, a click, or "Show everything". `harness/core/t_goto_all.py` and `run_goto_parallel.py` check every node with a mesh or hotspot.
- **Two bodies.** `FT.body` (js/nodes.js) builds both people from options: the man is `FR.body` (data/anatomy/, BodyParts3D scaled to 1.75 m, at the park's origin), the woman is `FR.body_f` (opts `{anat: 'data/anatomy_f/', prefix: 'vhf_', who: 'woman'}`, the Human Reference Atlas female set, not scaled, 1.2 m to his right). In a frame a part keeps its plain id (`F.parts.skin`, `hidden`, `sel`, the descent tables); only the world-wide mesh registry (`regMesh`) carries her prefix, and `nodeForMesh` strips it. Each part of hers may name its card (`card` in her index; `ANAT_CARD`). Her skin file is fetched at once, the rest when the camera is within 4 m of her, when something of hers is asked for (`F.loadAll`), or after 15 s; `F.readyP`/`partsReady` mean everything is in. Her index also carries the markers of her organ views measured on her parts (`hotspots`; markers she has no part for go to the nearest point of her organ), two views of her own (`organ_views`: the uterus with its tubes, ovaries and ligaments; her left breast), the region of every skin vertex (`regions`, tools/anatomy/female/regions_f.py: her model has no arm, hand, foot or skull bones) and the man's parts her atlas lacks (`absent`, checked against the source files by name). `goToNode` picks the body (`bodyFor`: the one the camera is in, else the nearer, else the man; the other one when only it has the structure); `goToNodeIn(id, who)` names one. js/body_two.js adds "In this model" to a body card (where her part's shape comes from, "See it in the man/woman", "Not in her model"). The realistic look keeps per-body ambient occlusion and skin zones; the detailed skin (skin_hd.bin) and the park shadow proxy are the man's only. Rebuild her data: `WORK=work/anatomy_f sh tools/anatomy/female/fetch.sh && python -I tools/anatomy/female/build_f.py --work work/anatomy_f && node tools/anatomy/bake_ao.mjs data/anatomy_f`; pictures to check: `python tools/anatomy/female/preview_f.py data/anatomy_f OUT`. Tests: harness/core/t_two_bodies.py (`--goto` for every card of her parts).
- **Lungs.** The anatomy data holds the lungs as airway trees only, so `lungShellGeo` wraps each tree in a schematic outline (a star-shaped hull of the tree's reach, pushed out of the heart). It is a child mesh of the body part (`userData.shell`) and a clickable wall in the cut-open view; the card and view say it is schematic (`LUNG_NOTE`).
- **The park from above and from its edge** (the `park` wrapper in js/world_matter.js). The painted lawn (170 m) goes on to 520 m as a ring that fades at its rim, and an unseen ring to 3 km answers the pointer: below the horizon there is ground wherever you point. The hills are cut at the ground, painted as lawn and are ground to a click. The sky is never a zoom pivot (`noZoom`: with the pointer on it the zoom closes on the point of air in front of the camera), is drawn first and is centred on the camera. From above 300 m a zoom homes in on the middle of the park (`attract` with `attractMin`). The park's dot on the globe floats 6 km up, so it says where a zoom aimed at it ends (`userData.zoomPoint(h)`, read by `pivotAt`); a region does not put generic grassland in the way of a zoom whose pivot is in the park. Map tiles and landscapes are `landOn`: with no surface under the pointer the target sinks to the ground in step with the zoom (`applyZoom`). A wheel burst keeps its pivot when the surface under a resting pointer goes away (a tree's far shape giving way to leaves: `zoomBy`).
- **Landmarks** are `{label, D, frame, at?, dir?, go(then), side?}`. `at` is the place in the frame when that is not its middle; `dir` (frame coordinates, from the target to the camera) is the direction to look from on arrival (`flyTo(frame, p, D, then, dir)` turns the view as it flies: the Inner planets and Solar system stops look down on the plane of the orbits). A thing's ruler stop gets the thing's frame in `buildWorld`. `frame` is where the camera must end up; `harness/core/t_all_landmarks.py` checks every landmark and every `placeGo` kind. Place names never show raw ids (`humanName`).
- **Tours** walk the non-`side` landmarks one at a time (`startTour`, `nextTour`, tour bar); `cancelTour()` is called by every manual input.
- **Layered frames (things).** Any frame with `layered = true`, `parts`, `hidden`, `sel`, `reveal` gets the body's core sample, "Hide this", "Peel to here" and "Show everything" (`showBodyNode` reads `h.frame.layered`); `F.coreIds()` / `F.coreClip(point)` / `F.coreText` narrow the sample to what is in the current detail tier and not cut away. `js/things.js` (`FT.thing`) is the user: its Auto peel (`thPeel`, `thAutoUpdate`) is `bodyPeel` over the thing's `layers`; its pick rule is `thChooseHit`. Extension points in `js/nodes.js`: `CARD_HOOKS`, `GOTO_HOOKS`, `frame.cardMadeOf`. The hover tooltip is worked out with `PICK_HOVER` set (`js/body_core.js`): when only see-through layers are under the cursor it names nothing instead of the nearest ghost. Details: `tools/things/SCHEMA.md` section 9.
- **Going to a part of a thing** (`thFindNode`, `thFlyToNode`, js/things.js). The flight aims at the part's centre (a point on the part when its centre is off the surface; the middle of its paths for a flow part), or, for an instanced part (a leaf, a petal, a hair), at ONE representative instance chosen the same way every time (`thRepInstance`: within reach, outermost). The nearest it goes is `part.fly_m`, else 0.4 m scaled with the thing but at most 4 radii of a small part. `F.spawnHold` stops a flight that ends very close from diving into what the part is made of until you zoom in further. On landing a cut or pivoted view is turned to face the camera, and `thClearView` looks from another side if something of another frame (or, a moment later, a solid part of the thing's own) hides the target. A card about something inside a thing that has no visible marker peels what is in front of the nearest inner part. `noPick` on an object's `userData` makes it unpickable (the park's ground and grass while a thing has made them see-through); `auto.ground_see_through` lets the ray-based Auto do that.
- **Things anywhere** (js/things.js, "placing things: in the park, and anywhere else"; `tools/things/SCHEMA.md` section 10). A placement in `data/things/index.json` may name where the thing stands with `in`: `phone` (with `part`, the teardown part it replaces), `body`, `organ:<id>`, `earth` (`lat`, `lon`), `orbit`, `floating`, or any key of `FR`. A resolver (`TH_HOSTS.<name>`) turns the placement into a host frame and a position; `thPlace` makes the frame. The rule: a thing's frame takes its host's unit (`T.s = k / unit` scales the model, which stays in metres) and is co-rendered with the host, so tiers, layers, cuts, picking, flights and the descent are the same code as in the park. The host's plain stand-in is the frame's `proxy` (hidden by the engine while the thing is shown). `FT.phone` opens up, fades its other parts and stops drawing the hand while the camera is in a thing inside it (`F.inK`). A `floating` thing stands on `FT.thstage` (neutral ground, scale bar, a note that it is out of context, a zoom-out stop). A thing outside the park is disposed after 25 s without being drawn (`thSweep`) and built again on return. `harness/core/t_things_anywhere.py` covers the cell in the phone and each kind of place.
- **Flights to parts, three rules** (js/things.js). A hotspot marker is never drawn wider than about 12 pixels (`TH_HOT_PX`, in `ThingObj.update`). A flight to a part of a thing that stands on a ground never ends beneath the ground (`thAboveGround`, used by `thFlyToNode`, `thModeView` and `thClearView`); with a view cut along a fixed plane the camera stands in front of the cut face. A part made of several separate small pieces is flown to at one piece (`thRepPiece`), and the distance to one of a crowd is about six of its own sizes (`thOneD`), not a quarter of a metre.

## What everything is made of (the descent)

The aim: point at anything, keep zooming, and arrive at what it is made of, level by level, down to the quarks. Three pieces make that work, and none of them is per-thing code.

### 1. A descent ends ON something (attractors, `js/engine.js`)

A frame may name the points a zoom can end on: `F.att = { D, find(P, hint) -> id, pos(id, out) -> out, tol? }` (the comment above `attLock` has the contract). Below the camera distance `D` the zoom locks on to the attractor nearest the pointer's pivot (or the one the pointer is on). From then on the camera is scaled about that point, and the target closes on it as the square of the zoom: nothing jumps, only the centre of the zoom changes. `attId(F)` tells a spawn function which attractor the zoom is locked on, so the frame that opens is the one it converged on. While a zoom is locked, a fixed child frame further than `tol` from the attractor is not entered (the fixed oxygen atom of the DNA scene does not capture a zoom that is closing on its neighbour).

| frame | attractors | from |
|---|---|---|
| molecule, crystal, liquid water (`atomAtt`, `js/world_matter.js`) | every atom | 2.5 nm |
| gas | every molecule | 8 nm |
| plasma | every nucleus (not the electrons) | 10 nm; the particles come to rest as you close in |
| atom (`js/atom.js`) | the nucleus | as soon as the camera is in the atom (it waited until 120 pm, and a pointer off the middle took the zoom out of the cloud) |
| nucleus (`js/particles.js`) | every nucleon | as soon as the camera is in the frame |
| proton or neutron | the three valence quarks; the Standard Model view opens at the one the zoom closed on | as soon as the camera is in the frame |

The older single-point form (`F.attract`, `attractD`, `attractR`) still works (the park in its region, the Galactic Centre). Spawn functions of these frames never return null for want of precision: a molecule returns the nearest atom however far the target is from it.

### 1b. The zoom's target (`ztg`, `js/engine.js`)

A wheel burst, a pinch or a held zoom button that begins on a thing keeps that thing as the zoom's **target** until the pointer moves more than `ZT_PX`
(6 px), a drag, a zoom out or a flight. Every frame of the zoom the pivot is moved to the thing (never the camera): its centre while it is under a
tenth of the view, the point picked on it from a fifth up, blended between; the point is kept in the coordinates of the object hit, so a moving thing
is followed. Later picks only refine the point on the same thing (in a thing that peels its layers, also a deeper part inside its extent; a layer in
front never takes it back). `lastZoomHit` names the target, so the descent goes into it, and a dot that stands for a frame leads into that frame.
Rules on top: `ztUnder` (a small organ within the forgiving pick's ring, under the surface the pointer is on, is the target: the eye, not the brain
behind the brow), `ztHandOn` (a thing with `aimHolds` hands the aim to the layer an Auto stage shows), `ztg.carry` (a scene that is one material
throughout, `DESCENT.fills`, goes on into that material at the same point). A frame's attractors still decide inside it. Empty space has a material
too: `empty` and `emptyD` in an assign file (the solar wind, interstellar gas) are where a zoom aimed at nothing goes.

### 2. The table: `data/descent/materials.json`

A **material** is a reusable description of matter at one level:

```json
"bone": { "title": "Bone", "desc": "one plain line", "card": "bone_tissue",
          "inside": [ { "name": "compact bone (osteons)", "scene": "tissue:bone", "note": "what is to scale and what is not" } ],
          "src": "where the statement comes from" }
```

- `inside` lists what you find one level down. The first entry with a `scene` (or a `material`) is the way down; entries with neither name what else is in it and has no scene yet (they are listed on screen as "Also in it, not built yet").
- `scene` names an existing reusable scene: `tissue2:<id>` (a file in `data/things/tissues`), `tissue:<kind>` (the twelve `FT.tissue` kinds), `cell:<animal|plant|bacterium|fungal>`, `micro:<kind>`, `mol:<id>`, `crystal:<id>`, `liquid`, `gas`, `gas:giant`, `plasma`, `atom:<El>`, `dna`; or the object form with parameters (`{"type":"micro","kind":"metal","title":"Copper, magnified","crystal":"copper_fcc"}`; `{"type":"gas","mix":[["H2",0.9],["He",0.1]],"title":...,"note":...}`; `{"type":"cell","cell":"plant","cname":"..."}`; a micro scene may carry `material`: what its grains, surface, water or gas lead into).
- `material` instead of `scene` goes on through another material ("sap" is "water", with a note).
- `standIn: true` with a `note` says what is shown in place of what. `{what}` / `{What}` in a `title` or `note` is replaced by the name of the part that was pointed at. The caption of the scene starts with "A stand-in." and the note.
- `stop: "sentence"` instead of `inside`: nothing true can be shown below (a marker, the sky, a far bird, glass, the leaf's wax). The camera stays at the frame's floor and the sentence is shown.
- `card` is the node that explains the material (`null` when there is none yet).

`node tools/descent/check.mjs` proves the table: every card and scene exists; every material resolves, through other materials and through the structures of the tissue scenes it opens, to a molecule, crystal, liquid, gas, plasma or atom, or is an honest stop; no cycle; no dangling id; a stand-in has a note; every structure of every tissue scene has a `deeper` or a `material`. Run it after every change to the table, an assignment file or a tissue scene.

### 3. Assignment files: `data/descent/assign/<frame type or thing id>.json`

They say which material each pickable thing of a frame is made of (the file must be listed under `assign` in `materials.json`):

```json
{ "default": "animal_cells",
  "systems": { "skeleton": "bone", "muscles": "skeletal_muscle" },
  "ids":     { "claws": "keratin", "teeth": "tooth", "mu_canal": null },
  "nodes":   { "dog_costal": "cartilage" },
  "match":   [ ["^(hs_|at_)", "marker"] ],
  "sub":     { "skin": { "ids": { "microsurface": "corneocytes" } } },
  "spawnD": 0.008, "everywhere": true }
```

Looked up in this order: the part's pick id (`ids`), its card (`nodes`), the first regular expression that matches its id (`match`), its system (`systems`), then `default`. `over` (pick id to material) comes before all of them, and before the part's own `deeper` and `material` in the thing's data: it corrects a generated model without regenerating it (assign/dog.json sends the dog's tendons, which the model gives the muscle scene, to the tendon scene). `sub` holds the same structure per kind of the frame (a tissue kind, a cell type, a micro kind, a landscape's body or biome) and is tried first. A tissue scene object may carry a `variant` (`{"type":"tissue","kind":"epithelium","variant":"lining"}`): its `sub` is looked up by the variant, so the same picture can mean something else (the lining of a windpipe has no dead top layer). `over: {structureId: material}` inside a `sub` (assign/tissue2.json, keyed by scene id) replaces the `material` a tissue scene's own file gives that structure. `null` means "nothing is assigned to this part" (the default does not apply). `default` speaks for whatever of the frame is pointed at; `everywhere: true` makes it hold where the pointer is on nothing too (a chip is silicon throughout). `spawnD` (metres) is the camera distance below which a frame without a spawn function of its own asks the table (default: four times its floor).

### 4. The rule (`js/descent.js`, hooks in `js/engine.js`)

When the camera descends at a point and the frame it is in has nothing more specific there (no child frame; its own `spawn` returns null), `trySpawn` asks `DESCENT.spawn(frame, T, hint)`. The material of the thing under the zoom point is, in this order: the part's own `material` (a thing part or a tissue-scene structure may carry one in its data); the row of the assignment file that names it; `userData.madeOf` when that is a material id; the file's default. The material's scene is created at that point and follows the zoom target until the camera reaches it. Frames that have a spawn function of their own ask the table first, but only for rows that name the part (`DESCENT.known`): the body (`assign/body.json`), cut-open organs (`organ.json`), `FT.tissue` scenes (`tissue.json`), the cell (`cell.json`), micro scenes (`micro.json`), the park (`park.json`), landscapes (`landscape.json`); a thing's part with its own `deeper` keeps it, every other part goes through the thing's file.

- **Which part.** A thing: the part the pointer is on while it is still showing, else the part that is visibly under the target (`thPartAt`); a peeled, see-through layer never answers. A tissue scene: the structure the pointer is on, else the nearest structure, so a descent never ends in the gap between two cells. A cell: the organelle pointed at; being near the nucleus counts only when the pointer is on nothing.
- **Which frame.** A co-rendered child (a thing in the park, the body) is drawn and picked with its parent, so when the zoom's pivot is on the parent's own content or on another thing, the camera does not go down into it (`zoomAim`): the grass beside the oak leads into soil, not into the oak. A frame may add its own condition with `enterIf(aim)` (the phone lies in the hand: the skin leads to it, a hand bone does not).
- **Floors.** `Frame.floorD` holds only where nothing lies below: a child frame waiting under the target lifts it (`zoomFloor`), so the zoom runs on into that frame. A `stop` makes the place where it was said a floor, also in a frame that has none; where a floor holds and nothing has been said, `DESCENT.floorSay` says that the picture ends there. A failed spawn is tried again at most every 0.4 s, or when the target or the pointed object changes or the camera has come a sixth closer (nothing here runs per frame).
- **Waiting frames.** A scene made at the zoom target waits, following the target, until the camera is close enough to enter it (`trySpawn`); it is dropped only when the camera has pulled back threefold from where it was made. One made while the pointer was on nothing (a frame-wide default) gives way when the pointer comes to rest on a thing. After the camera has entered a scene that is still a detail in the view, the scene keeps following the target until the view is down to its own size (`reanchor`), so a target that is still gliding toward the pointer does not leave it and make it again; and once the view is a fraction of a spawned scene's size, the camera leaves it only when the target is three radii out (a plant cell reaches past the radius its frame is given). Between a scene and the much smaller one below it (a landscape and a crystal, a galaxy and its gas) there is nothing to draw: the zoom runs on through the larger picture, faster and with a line saying so (Empty stretches, below), until the smaller one is reached, and double-click (`DESCENT.inside`) flies there directly.
- **Scroll and double-click on layered things.** Scrolling toward a thing with an Auto peel (the body, the dog, the bee) peels its layers, and the descent goes into the part that is visibly under the pointer when the camera arrives, as before. A double click in Auto takes one of those steps at a time (`layerStep`, see The panel, click and double-click). A thing whose Auto makes parts see-through by distance without cutting (`aimHolds`, set in `buildWorld` from `auto.stages`: the pond) keeps the part first pointed at as the aim of a wheel burst while it is still drawn under an unmoved pointer (`zoomBy`), and that part decides what opens (`DESCENT.spawnHeld`): scrolling at the pond's water ends in water, not in the mud that shows through it. `DESCENT.inside(hit)` goes into the part that was picked, whatever lies behind it; an object's `zoomTo` is not taken again when the camera is already where it leads (a second double click on the Moon goes on to its surface).
- **Honesty.** `decorate` puts the stand-in line in front of the scene's own note. A cell opened from a tissue is named for what it stands for ("Hepatocyte (liver cell): drawn as a generic animal cell").

## Co-rendering, fades, extension hooks

- **Co-rendered neighbours.** A child with `coRender = true` and the same `unit` as its parent is drawn whenever the parent is (out to `coMax` metres, default `r1`), shares the parent's depth buffer and is picked with it. If it also sets `mergeable = true` it is drawn inside the parent's scene, one render call for all of them (see Performance); a neighbour that is out of view or under about two pixels is not drawn, picked or (seven frames in eight) updated. `computeActive` builds `active` (the camera chain) and `drawn` (chain plus neighbours). `coAlpha(c, inChain)` is its visibility: the parent's alpha times a smooth distance fade (`0.7*coMax .. coMax`); a co-rendered frame in the camera chain gets only the distance fade, not the parent's `r0` fade. **Neighbours get out of the way of a close-up.** When the camera is anchored in (or inside) one co-rendered thing and within 5 sizes of it (`cam.D < 5 * radius`), every other co-rendered neighbour fades out with its distance from the camera: gone beyond 24 times the viewing distance, untouched inside 10 times it (`coAlpha`, js/engine.js, `_coHost`). At the bee (D 1 cm) the person 4 m away is not drawn at all; pulling back past 5 sizes brings everything back smoothly. It is general: no trail or card has to hide the body (the trails' `hideBody` action still works and is still needed only for the phone, which sits in the hand of the body itself). The body does its own opacity per layer (`applyMode` multiplies `F.alpha`), so it is `noFade` and still fades out smoothly; its `coMax` (400 m) is inside the park's 420 m sky dome. A co-rendered neighbour's own co-rendered children are drawn with it, one level down (`coKids`): a thing placed in the human body is seen from the park. `reanchor` never disposes a frame that is in `drawn`; `safePlace` guards `place()` of frames not built yet; flight callbacks run through `safeThen`.
- **Extension hooks.** `WORLD_EXT.push(async FR => {...})` runs inside `buildWorld()` after the fixed frames exist; `EXT_LANDMARKS.push({label, D, frame, go(then), side?})` adds a ruler stop (give it `frame` so the landmark test can check it); `FT.<type> = {...}` defines a frame type; `SEARCH_SOURCES.push(source)` adds search results (below).

### Rules

- **No per-frame raycasts.** Hover is worked out only after the pointer has moved and rested (70 ms) or the view under it changed (`viewChanged()`), never while the camera moves. A wheel burst re-uses its pivot. Anything that casts rays from an update hook must be keyed on what it depends on and rate-limited, as `bodyAutoUpdate` is (camera position and target, at most every 110 ms).
- **Picking is accelerated; keep it that way.** Every mesh of 64 triangles or more gets a bounding-volume tree (three-mesh-bvh, `js/vendor/`), built in idle slices (`pkIdle`); an `InstancedMesh` gets per-instance bounds in a tree (`pkInstAccel`) and is tested nearest instance first, stopping after four hits that are not cut away by the material's clipping planes (so only the nearest hits of a crowd are returned); a frame's pickables are skipped when the ray misses their bounding sphere. A geometry whose positions are rewritten every frame, or that uses `setDrawRange`, is tested the plain way: keep such geometry small or out of `pickables`. An object with its own `raycast` method switches the frame's sphere test off.
- **Opaque by default.** `illoMat` materials are opaque while their opacity is 1 and blend only while something lowers it. Do not create a material with `transparent: true` unless it is always see-through, additive, or part of a construction that relies on render order (the cut-open organ parts, the markers). Layers that must be painted in a fixed order whatever their depth use `paintedInOrder` (the body).
- **No per-frame geometry rebuilds and no allocations in update hooks.** Reuse vectors and matrices; move things in the vertex shader where you can (the things' sway).
- **Instance crowds.** Anything that repeats more than a few dozen times is an `InstancedMesh`. Give the frame a detail tier that drops it with distance.
- **Three detail tiers** for anything large (`lod` in a thing's data: near, mid, far), and no ink on the far tier.
- **Static parts set `matrixAutoUpdate = false`** (after `updateMatrix()`).
- **One scene where possible.** A frame type that is co-rendered with its parent and can live with the parent's lights sets `mergeable = true` and hides its own lights in `onMerge(on)`; the engine then draws it inside the parent's scene. A frame that needs its own lights (the body) stays a scene of its own.
- **Glow is opt-out by nature of the scene:** an illustrated frame (`illo`) seen close and a daylight frame that fills the view (`daylight`) take none; then no full-screen pass runs at all.
- **Shadows:** a shadow-casting light sets `shadow.autoUpdate = false` and asks for an update only when the light or a caster has moved (see `shadowStep` in `js/world_matter.js`, which also shifts the shadow matrix with the scene origin).
- **Lights do not swim.** Scenes are shifted so the eye is at the origin, and three.js takes a hemisphere light's direction from its position in the scene; `fixSceneLights` (js/engine.js, in `placeFrameCam`) moves each hemisphere light (and each shadowless directional light without a target) by the opposite shift, so a hemisphere light always points the way the frame gave it (up) wherever the camera is. A scene's own sun that casts a shadow is left alone.
- **Custom shaders** wrap GLSL with `LDV()`/`LDF()` as before; that keeps them right in all three depth modes.
- **Point sizes** in a shader multiply by the shared uniform `PIXK` (js/engine.js), so they keep their size on screen when the render scale adapts.

### What the engine does for you

- **Culling:** a co-rendered neighbour whose content is out of view or under about two pixels is not drawn or picked, and its update hook runs every eighth frame.
- **Render scale:** capped at about 2.8 million pixels and adapted to the frame time (`RENDER SCALE` in js/engine.js). Under browser automation it is fixed unless `dpr=auto` is in the URL.
- **Resting:** after 1.5 s with nothing moving, every other frame is skipped; hidden tabs and open labs draw nothing.
- **Shader compile:** a frame built before it is first drawn (the path of a flight) has its shaders compiled then (`compileStep`), so the stall comes at the click, not mid-flight. Hidden variants (other layer modes, cut views) compile when first shown: compiling blocks in three r128, and doing it ahead for everything cost seconds at start-up.
- **Loading:** anatomy files and thing bundles are fetched and decoded in `js/mesh_worker.js`; `loadNote(label, promise)` names what is still arriving.
